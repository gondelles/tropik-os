# RB_03_LA_GUAIRA_SITE_MVP_MODEL_v0.1

**Status:** PRIVATE_WORKING  
**Venture:** Tropik Re:Build  
**Updated:** 2026-09-06

## Executive decision

Re:Build should **not** start by building a standalone greenfield recycling plant.

The MVP should seek a **dedicated operating lane inside or immediately adjacent to an already authorized debris-management site** in La Guaira, with existing public/private trucks feeding the lane and major heavy machinery borrowed, shared or rented.

The MVP objective is to prove:

1. safe intake and traceability;
2. sorting discipline;
3. measurable recovery yield;
4. repeatable processing;
5. product qualification;
6. buyer/offtake pathways;
7. cost per tonne;
8. community-employment model.

It is **not** intended to solve the full 2.1M-tonne disaster-debris problem in 90 days.

# 1. Candidate sites — working shortlist

## Candidate 1 — Santa Eduvigis / Urimare

**Working role:** Primary MVP processing hub / final-node partnership.

### Why it ranks #1
- Publicly identified authorized final debris destination.
- Public reporting describes approximately 48 ha total facility area.
- Two terraces of approximately 5 ha and 6 ha have reportedly been prepared for debris handling/placement.
- Existing processing and government supervision reduce the burden of creating a new permit/site ecosystem from zero.
- Best candidate for a dedicated 0.5–1.5 ha Re:Build operating cell if authorities approve.

### Main risks
- Re:Build may not receive legal authority to operate commercially inside the site.
- Material ownership and ability to sell recovered outputs may be restricted.
- Existing public operations may already consume all useful space/equipment.
- Final-disposal function may conflict with keeping high-value recovered material segregated.

### Preliminary score
**82/100 — preferred field-validation candidate.**

# 2. Recommended network architecture

### MVP architecture

`AUTHORIZED SOURCE`
→ existing truck/loading system
→ **nearest authorized CDT / Re:Build intake lane**
→ safety screening + gross sorting
→ reusable components/metals removed
→ mineral fraction
→ crushing + screening at shared/mobile processing node
→ sampled/tested product stockpile
→ approved reconstruction use / buyer
→ residuals to authorized final disposal.

### Preferred physical topology

- **Caraballeda:** east collection/sorting satellite.
- **Catia La Mar:** west collection/sorting satellite.
- **Santa Eduvigis:** preferred central/final processing + QA + product stockpile hub.

This three-node model is a WORKING HYPOTHESIS. Re:Build should begin with **one hub only** if approval, equipment and feedstock routing are simpler.

# 3. Minimum site requirement

## Absolute MVP minimum

**5,000 m² / 0.5 ha** if:
- only one major processing line operates;
- incoming stock is limited;
- outbound product moves quickly;
- residuals leave regularly;
- trucks have one-way or carefully controlled circulation;
- hazardous/suspect material is quarantined in a small dedicated zone and removed promptly.

## Preferred MVP

**10,000–15,000 m² / 1.0–1.5 ha.**

This provides safer truck maneuvering, stockpile separation, product QA areas and some growth buffer.

## Do not accept a site merely because land is available

The site must support the following zones:

| Zone | Preferred MVP area | Purpose |
|---|---:|---|
| Entry / control / scale lane | 400–700 m² | logging, inspection, truck queue |
| Tipping / incoming inspection | 600–1,000 m² | unload + visual screening |
| Suspect / quarantine zone | 200–400 m² | isolated material only |
| Reuse/personal-effects hold | 200–400 m² | controlled recovery pathway |
| Metal separation | 300–500 m² | ferrous/non-ferrous stock |
| Clean concrete/mineral feed | 1,000–1,500 m² | crusher feedstock |
| Mixed masonry/mineral feed | 700–1,000 m² | secondary mineral stream |
| Crusher + screen safety envelope | 1,000–1,500 m² | mobile processing train |
| Finished product stockpiles | 1,000–2,000 m² | separated graded outputs |
| Residual/non-recoverable | 300–600 m² | short-dwell residuals |
| Water/dust/environment controls | 200–400 m² | tank, pump, sediment controls |
| Workshop/fuel/service | 200–400 m² | controlled maintenance area |
| Welfare/container office | 100–200 m² | staff, records, first aid |
| Internal roads/turning/buffers | balance | safe movement |

The exact footprint depends on local setbacks, drainage and public-site rules.

# 4. Non-negotiable site conditions

## Location / access
- Authorized land use and written operating permission.
- All-weather truck access.
- Stable load-bearing surface for 20–30 t vehicles/equipment.
- Space for truck turning without reversing through worker sorting zones.
- Not in a known floodplain or active drainage path if avoidable.
- Preferably outside dense residential frontage.

## Environmental controls
- No uncontrolled runoff toward sea, rivers or storm drains.
- Perimeter drainage / sediment control.
- Dust suppression using water mist/sprays.
- Stabilized truck exit to reduce track-out.
- Covered/contained fine material where practical.
- Spill kits and controlled fuel/service area.
- Fire extinguishers + fire plan.

## Material controls
- Separate incoming, processed and rejected stockpiles.
- Physical quarantine area for suspected asbestos, chemicals, fuel-contaminated material, medical waste, unknown drums or other hazardous material.
- No processing of quarantined material by Re:Build.
- Clear signs for accepted/not-accepted materials.

## Security / governance
- Staffed during operating hours.
- Controlled entry after hours.
- Daily intake/output log.
- Photo record and load-ticket traceability.
- Chain of custody for personal effects / sensitive recovered items.

# 7. Processing sequence

## Stage 0 — dispatch authorization
Debris is routed only from an approved source under the authorized recovery command structure.

## Stage 1 — arrival and logging
Record:
- truck ID;
- source zone;
- date/time;
- gross/net weight or validated load estimate;
- photographs;
- declared material type.

**Working time:** 5–15 minutes/load if no queue.

## Stage 2 — safety inspection
Reject/quarantine suspect hazardous material and isolate personal/sensitive effects.

**Working time:** 5–15 minutes/load plus specialist hold when required.

## Stage 3 — tipping + gross sort
Separate:
- reusable components;
- steel/metals;
- clean concrete;
- masonry/mineral;
- wood/fiber where clean;
- plastics/other recyclables;
- residuals;
- suspect material.

**Working time:** continuous; mixed loads can add substantial labor.

## Stage 4 — pre-processing
Remove reinforcement, large metal and unsuitable contamination. Break oversized mineral pieces only as needed for safe crusher feed.

## Stage 5 — crushing
Mobile jaw crusher reduces mineral material.

## Stage 6 — magnetic separation
Recover ferrous metal before/after primary crush depending equipment configuration.

## Stage 7 — screening
Separate target gradations for defined uses.

## Stage 8 — QC / sampling
Measure:
- source;
- batch weight;
- particle-size distribution;
- contamination;
- moisture where relevant;
- product-specific laboratory properties.

## Stage 9 — stockpile / release
Keep each product batch physically separate until release for its approved use.

## Stage 10 — sale / redeployment
Load approved material to buyer/reconstruction project and record weight/destination.

# 8. Working processing times

These are planning assumptions, not guaranteed performance.

| Material/batch | Working processing time | Calendar release time |
|---|---|---|
| Clean concrete/mineral load | same-day sorting/crushing possible | 0.5–1 day before stockpile; longer if lab release required |
| 20 t clean truckload | ~0.5–2 processing hours across steps once line is running | same day |
| 50 t clean mineral batch | ~4–8 operating hours | 1 day + QA |
| Mixed C&D load | intensive sorting; variable | ~1–3 days from intake to segregated processed stock |
| First new product qualification | n/a | ~2–4 weeks after representative sample exists |
| Routine graded product after method is validated | same-day processing | same day to 1–2 days depending QC |

Crusher nameplate capacity should **not** be used as plant throughput. The real MVP bottleneck will likely be safe sorting, material consistency, truck timing, QA and outbound demand.

# 9. MVP throughput plan

## Why small relative to regional volume
UNDP reports ~2.1M t of debris. A Re:Build MVP should optimize learning per dollar, not maximize tonnage before product/offtake validation.

## 90-day ramp

### Month 1 — controlled commissioning
**TARGET:** 25 t/day x 24 days = **600 t**.
- approximately 30 x 20-t truckloads for the month;
- learn intake, sort categories, contamination and mass balance;
- produce first mineral product batches;
- qualify buyers/labs.

### Month 2 — repeatability
**TARGET:** 50 t/day x 24 days = **1,200 t**.
- approximately 60 x 20-t truckloads/month;
- stabilize recovery yield;
- execute at least one buyer/reconstruction trial.

### Month 3 — controlled scale
**TARGET:** 75 t/day x 24 days = **1,800 t**.
- approximately 90 x 20-t truckloads/month;
- prove product consistency and operating economics.

### 90-day total
**TARGET:** **3,600 t**, approximately **180 x 20-t truckloads**.

This is only about **0.17%** of the publicly estimated 2.106M t debris volume. That is acceptable because the pilot milestone is evidence, not regional cleanup share.

# 10. Mass-balance model

For every 100 t received:

`100 t IN`
=
`reusable components`
+
`metals`
+
`processed mineral product`
+
`other recyclables`
+
`quarantined/specialist material`
+
`residual disposal`
+
`measurement variance/moisture`.

## Mandatory KPI

**Mass-balance closure:** target within ±3–5% after the weighing system is stabilized.

## Do not assume a 30% Re:Build recovery rate
A public expert estimate has suggested around 30% reuse potential for the overall debris context. Re:Build must measure its own batch yields by source and material class.

**MVP priority:** highest.  
**Gate:** grading + contamination + local engineering requirements.

## Product 2 — Recycled Mineral Fill
**Use:** approved non-structural fill/backfill/terracing applications.  
**MVP priority:** high.

**Use:** approved fill/base applications after testing.  
**MVP priority:** medium-high.

## Product 4 — Recovered Steel Scrap
**Use:** sale to recycler/industry.  
**MVP priority:** immediate where safe/clean.

**Use:** case-by-case salvage/reuse.

Mycelium-bound recovered fiber/mineral prototype for non-structural applications. **Not part of MVP economics.**

# 13. MVP approved / not approved

## GO to physical pilot only if
- [ ] written site authorization;
- [ ] authorized debris routing;
- [ ] HSE/environment procedure approved;
- [ ] external hazardous-waste pathway confirmed;
- [ ] loader/excavator/crusher/screen availability documented;
- [ ] weighing method available;
- [ ] at least one proposed mineral use has a testing protocol;
- [ ] at least one buyer/reconstruction project agrees to evaluate pilot material;
- [ ] funded worker/PPE/operating budget exists for the pilot period;
- [ ] ownership/use rights for recovered outputs are legally clear.

## HOLD
Site exists but material rights, equipment, HSE or buyer path remain unresolved.

## REDESIGN
Re:Build would need to buy major plant before it can validate the process, cannot obtain an authorized site/feedstock, or cannot legally commercialize/redeploy recovered material.
