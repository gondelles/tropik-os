## 8. TECHNICAL STUDY — RB-03A update

**STRATEGIC DECISION:** one-way controlled process: authorized dispatch -> weigh/photo -> safety inspection -> tipping -> sorting -> reuse/metals/mineral streams -> oversize reduction -> crushing -> magnetic separation -> screening -> QA/hold -> release -> dispatch -> residual authorized disposal.

Exception flows are mandatory for contaminated/suspect loads, personal/sensitive items and hazardous material. Re:Build does not process hazardous waste.

**TARGET:** accepted clean truck turnaround 30–45 min absent external queue; mixed 45–60 min.  
**WORKING HYPOTHESIS:** effective processing 15–40 t/h is sufficient at MVP scale; crusher nameplate is not plant throughput.  
**TARGET:** mass-balance closure ±3–5% after stabilized weighing.

Detailed process/layout: `40_REBUILD/operations/RB_03A_MVP_SITE_LAYOUT_v0.1.md`.

## 10. INFRASTRUCTURE — RB-03A update

- **Minimum:** 5,000 m² only for 25–50 t/day, one line, low stock dwell and fast outbound.
- **Base design:** 10,000 m² exact reconciled layout.
- **Preferred:** 10,000–15,000 m².
- **Expansion:** 20,000–30,000 m² for ~300 t/day / larger buffers / two-shift development.

The 1-ha model reserves 1,150 m² expansion inside the cell and assigns the balance across controlled entry/weighing, inspection/tipping, quarantine, sorting/reuse/metals, mineral feed, crusher/screen, finished product, QA/dispatch, equipment/service, HSE/welfare and traffic/buffers.

## 27. SENSITIVITY — first priority

Stress variables in this order:
1. saleable mineral yield;
2. ferrous/non-ferrous content and realized scrap price;
3. equipment cash cost/uptime;
4. output price and buyer volume;
5. residual/suspect percentage and disposal fee;
6. truck/haul responsibility;
7. fuel;
8. labor productivity;
9. site cost;
10. testing cost/turnaround.

### Mineral-yield stress
LOW planning composition contains 52 t concrete+masonry/100 t received; at 70% saleable conversion this yields only **36.4 t saleable mineral/100 t**. BASE gives **55.25 t/100 t**. HIGH gives **67.16 t/100 t**. A material drop in mineral quality can therefore raise cash cost per saleable tonne sharply even when received tonnes stay constant.
